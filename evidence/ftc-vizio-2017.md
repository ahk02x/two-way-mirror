# FTC and New Jersey v. VIZIO (2017)

**The case that establishes this is real, in a court, with a penalty.**

- **Filed:** 6 February 2017
- **Court:** United States District Court for the District of New Jersey
- **Plaintiffs:** Federal Trade Commission, and the State of New Jersey / New Jersey Division of
  Consumer Affairs
- **Defendants:** VIZIO, Inc. and VIZIO Inscape Services, LLC
- **Outcome:** Stipulated order. $2.2 million in total payments. No admission of liability, which is
  standard in FTC settlements.
- **Primary source:** FTC case page, "VIZIO, Inc." — ftc.gov, Case 1:17-cv-00758

## What the complaint alleged

- VIZIO collected **second-by-second viewing data** from approximately **11 million** televisions.
- The data covered **all sources**: cable, broadcast, streaming apps, DVD, and **devices connected
  over HDMI** — meaning a games console or a laptop, not only television programming.
- The feature was **turned on by default**, and was **added by firmware update to televisions
  already sold**, whose owners had bought a TV that did not have it.
- Viewing data was **matched to IP address and then appended with demographic information** obtained
  from third parties — including sex, age, income, marital status, household size, education level,
  home ownership and home value.
- The combined data was **sold to third parties** for audience measurement and for targeting
  advertising to other devices in the same household.

## What the order required

- Prominent disclosure of the data collection, separate from any privacy policy or terms of service
- **Affirmative express consent** — opt-in, not opt-out, not bundled
- Deletion of data collected before 1 March 2016
- A privacy programme subject to biennial third-party assessment

## Why it matters beyond VIZIO

Read the separate written statement filed by the Commissioners alongside the settlement (linked
from the FTC case page — check the attribution there before quoting it). Its notable argument is
that **individualised television viewing history is sensitive information**, and that collecting it
without consent is a harm in itself, not only when it leads to some further injury.

That framing is the reason every other manufacturer now shows you a consent screen. They did not
become principled. They read this case.

**What this case does not establish:** that other manufacturers did the same thing without consent.
Post-2017, the industry moved to consent screens — which is why this repo is largely about finding
and reversing consent you technically gave, rather than about undisclosed collection.
