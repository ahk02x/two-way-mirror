# Evidence

The claims in this repo are not "some people say TVs spy on you." They are documented in court
filings, regulatory settlements, SEC disclosures, and peer-reviewed measurement research. This
folder holds the receipts.

| File | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| [ftc-vizio-2017.md](ftc-vizio-2017.md) | Two regulators sued, the manufacturer settled and paid, and the order set the opt-in rule |
| [vizio-sec-filings.md](vizio-sec-filings.md) | A manufacturer told its investors the data is worth more than the TV |
| [research.md](research.md) | Independent measurement of what these devices actually transmit |

## A standard for this folder

Every claim here should be traceable to a primary source a reader can open. If you add something:

- **Link the primary document**, not a news article about it. News articles link-rot and paraphrase.
- **Quote the specific finding**, do not summarise it into something stronger than it says.
- **Date it.** "TVs do X" is a claim about a moment. Vendors change.
- **Flag what you have not verified.** A citation marked *needs verification* is more useful than a
  confident number that turns out to be wrong.

The reason to hold this line is practical. The single fastest way to make this repo useless is for
one number in it to be wrong, because then every other number in it becomes arguable. Accuracy is
not a virtue here, it is the whole product.
